# Stage 5: Your first posts (how to make each kind)

Kristin's Create method: hooks, short video formats, the Reel script, the Reel checklist, the lead post, Stories, speaking on camera and captions.
Use this whenever you make a post with them. Every post starts with an idea from their idea bank, a hook from `hooks.md`, and their buyer's words.

**Their first 3 posts** (once the profile is live):
1. **Who I help:** an intro. Who they are, who they help, and what they'll share here. (Story pillar, nurture.)
2. **Their best quick tip:** the most useful thing they know about their buyer's problem. (Education pillar, growth.)
3. **Why I started:** their real story, in a few lines. (Story pillar, nurture.)
Pin them until better posts replace them.

## The hook (Kristin's findings for short video)

Write hooks from `hooks.md`, their big list and "Their words" (`stage-pillars-study.md`). Then put each one through these three checks.

### 1. Every nurture or sales hook makes one of 4 moves
Each post takes their buyer one step along their journey. A hook needs at least one of these (growth hooks don't have to):
1. **Meet them where they are:** what they're feeling, going through or stuck on right now. *"You're not bad at dinner. Deciding at 5 p.m. is the hard part."*
2. **Name what they want:** the result they truly wish for. *"Dinner on the table in 15 minutes, with no complaints."*
3. **Show the way from A to B:** what it takes to get there. *"5 things I changed to stop the 5 p.m. dinner panic."*
4. **Answer a doubt before it comes up:** the belief that stops them. *"You don't need to meal prep all Sunday to have dinner ready every night."*

### 2. Specific beats vague
Kristin's test: *would their buyer stop for this, and what does it promise them?* A vague hook gets scrolled past. Make it stronger with:
- **a number** (true ones only), **a time frame**, **a specific result**, **a specific struggle in their buyer's words**, **a list**, or **a keyword** from their big list. More is better.
- **a strong word or two:** exactly, finally, actually, stop, without. Used lightly, they add energy.

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| "Dinner tips for busy moms" | "3 dinners you can make in 15 minutes, even when you're exhausted at 5 p.m." |
| "How to meal plan" | "Exactly how to FINALLY stop asking 'what's for dinner?' every night" |
| "Grocery shopping hacks" | "The one grocery list that cut my store trips to once a week" *(only if it's true)* |

### 3. Borrow a proven shape, never the words
The biggest version of this: **remake a Reel that's taking off** in their niche (`apify.md`, "Remake a viral Reel"). They send the link, and you remake it for their topic and buyer.
From their **Proven hooks and phrases** (stage 4): keep the shape of a hook that's working, and fill it with their topic and their keyword. A shape like *"This {thing} is helping me {result}"* becomes *"This 10-minute Sunday list is getting dinner on our table by 5:30"*, as long as it's true for them. Results, money and numbers in a hook are only ever their own, real ones (`rules.md`, rule 7).

## Reels and YouTube Shorts (short vertical video)

Short videos reach the most people who don't follow them yet. **They don't need to show their face.** YouTube Shorts can be up to 3 minutes (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877), but short is usually better for a beginner: 7 to 30 seconds.

### Kristin's 3 formats
1. **Transition reveal:** hook text on screen for about 3 seconds → a transition on the beat drop → the tip or list right away. Works faceless. The caption carries the hook, story, steps and call to action.
2. **Hook to caption:** 2 or 3 short lines on screen about the problem → point down, "Details below". The full how-to lives in the caption. The video just stops the scroll.
3. **Face or screen tutorial:** a bold 3-second opener → a quick zoom → teach in fast 1 to 2 second cuts. Their face, or a screen recording of Canva or the product.

### Kristin's 30-second script
**Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → Call to action**
- **Hook (0 to 3 s):** 3 options (education, story, pattern interrupt) from `hooks.md`. Pick the strongest. Show it on screen and say it. Drop any option that opens with a greeting ("Hey guys"), a lead-in ("In this video I'm going to show you…") or nothing specific. If all three are weak, the idea is missing one true, specific thing (a number, a time, a result): ask them for it before writing.
- **Problem (3 to 8 s):** one sentence naming the exact struggle, in their buyer's words.
- **Solution (8 to 18 s):** the tip, or the product with 2 or 3 specific things inside it.
- **Proof (18 to 25 s):** a real result, or "what you can expect" if they don't have one yet. Never invented.
- **Call to action (25 to 30 s):** exactly what to do and where. One ask, not two.
- **Keep it moving:** something new on screen every 3 or 4 seconds (a new shot, a zoom, the next text card). A shot that sits still for longer is where people leave.
- **End on the beginning:** the last line echoes a word or number from the hook, so the replay feels natural. Replays count as views.
- **One idea per Reel.** If the script has two, it's two Reels.

Write it **shot by shot: what they say | the text on screen | what to show.** Say whether it works faceless. Tone from their notes. In the first 6 weeks the call to action is follow, save, comment or the freebie, not the product (`stage-pillars-study.md`, the mix).

### Kristin's Reel checklist (before they post)
Kristin's findings: Reels that struggle are usually missing 2 or 3 of these. Check every one:
- **A visual or audio hook in the first second:** movement that catches the eye (a sped-up time-lapse of them working, a quick zoom, a hand reaching into the frame) or a sound.
- **A text hook on screen: 12 words or fewer.** The shorter the better. Longer only when the first 4 or 5 words are strong and specific on their own.
- **The hook text is up from the very first frame,** not after a pause. Any text after the hook: 6 words or fewer per card, so it's read at a glance.
- **Every word sits in the safe middle of the screen:** away from the top (their name and the audio), the bottom third (the caption covers it) and the right edge (the like, comment and share buttons). Never put the hook down where the caption sits.
- **Captions on everything they say** (Instagram and most editing apps can add them automatically). Most people watch muted first.
- **The text hook has at least one of these** (more is better): a number, a time frame, a specific result, a specific struggle, a list, a keyword. True numbers only.
- **It makes one of the 4 moves** (nurture and sales posts): where they are, what they want, the way from A to B, or a doubt answered.
- **Their fonts and colors** ("My look").
- **A call to action they can see:** on screen, in the first line of the caption, or both. The caption's first line is the only part people see before "more". Tell them exactly what to do:
  - **Follow:** "Follow @{handle} if you're trying to {goal}."
  - **Comment** (for free value): "Comment {word} and I'll send you {freebie}, to help you {result}."
  Remind them why they're there to help.

### The lead post (a nurture post with a free gift)
Kristin's favorite way to turn viewers into leads. **The goal: as many comments as possible,** because every comment is someone raising their hand.
- **A short, punchy hook** that shows a big win or promises to fix a big problem (the checklist above).
- **Proof on screen only if it's real and theirs** (a screenshot of a real result). Otherwise, skip it.
- **One simple, strong call to action:** *"Comment DINNER and I'll send you my 15-minute dinner list."*
- **A very short caption** (Captions, below).
- **The free gift can be simple.** A one-page checklist, a short Google Doc with bullet points, a 3-minute video, a few examples. It doesn't need to be a 10-page designed guide; it needs to really help. Their freebie from the DREAM Engine's Market step works perfectly.
- **How it's delivered:** everyone who comments gets the gift in their messages. While comments are few, they send it by hand (it starts real conversations). When it's more than they can answer, set up the comment-keyword tool (`stage-sell.md`, section 3).
- **What happens next:** the message links to their freebie's sign-up page, so the gift arrives by email and they're on the email list, where the gentle selling happens later. From week 7, the message can also mention the product in one friendly line.

### Talking on camera (Kristin's findings)
- **Talk to one person.** Picture their buyer and talk to just them, like a friend.
- **Use an outline, not a script.** Know the 3 points, then say them naturally. Scripts make people sound flat.
- **Smile, relax the face, open hands.** Energy shows.
- **Pause before the big point.** It sounds confident, and it gives the viewer a second to catch up.
- **Teach only what they've lived or tested.** If they don't have a story for it, they're not ready to teach it yet.
- Watch it back once and fix one thing next time.

### Faceless options
Screen recording (of Canva, their product or a how-to), hands in frame, the printed product on a table, B-roll of everyday life with text on screen, or a voice-over.

**Screen recording, Kristin's way** (from her recording lessons; walk them through only the one they need):
- **On an iPhone:** swipe down to open Control Center and tap the round record button. If it isn't there, tap **+** → **Add a Control** → **Screen Recording**. After a 3-second countdown it records; tap the red indicator → **Stop Recording**. It saves to Photos. Trim the start and end before posting. (Other phones: search "how to screen record on {their phone}".)
- **On a Mac:** press **Command + Shift + 5**. Choose **Record Entire Screen** or **Record Selected Portion**, and drag the dotted box to fit just the browser window (great for a Canva tutorial or a peek at their shop). **Options** sets a timer and the microphone. Click **Record**, then the stop button in the menu bar; the video saves to the desktop. (Windows: search "how to screen record on {their computer}".)
- **In Canva:** Canva can record the screen too, right inside a design.
- **Longer tutorials:** Kristin uses Descript, a separate recording and editing app with a free starting plan (check its current plan). Only for students making longer how-to videos.

## Stories (Instagram and Facebook)

Kristin's Stories findings, made simple for a beginner. People choose to tap a Story, so they're more open to a call to action there than in their feed.

**Their Story rhythm:**
- Story days per their plan (`stage-rhythm.md`). Aim for most days once it's a habit.
- **3 to 6 slides a day,** spaced out across the day so it feels like real life, not a batch.
- **Mix them:** everyday life (coffee, the school run, their desk), making the product, a quick tip, a poll or question, and sometimes the call to action. A mix keeps people watching.
- **The first slide is the thumbnail** for the day. It needs a reason to tap: curiosity, or a promise of something useful. A face or a hand in it works best; text on a plain background gets tapped past.
- **Close the loop.** If slide 1 asks a question, answer it by the end, with a small lesson.
- **A poll or question box at least once a week:** what are you working on, what's hard right now? The answers become content ideas and their buyer's words.
- **Put the sticker early,** by slide 3 or 4, not on the last one: fewer people are still watching each slide.
- **Which sticker does what:** a **poll** is the easiest tap (lots of replies); a **question box** collects their buyer's exact words (every answer is a hook, a caption, or a question to answer with a Reel); a **quiz** clears up a common mistake; a **link** is the only clickable spot besides the bio; a **countdown** lets people get a reminder for a launch or a live.
- **Don't share a feed post to Stories with no words.** Add one line on why it's worth a look.
- **Calls to action several times a week** once they're selling: the product, the freebie, or "reply {word}". In the first 6 weeks: the freebie, a reply, or a post.

**Designing a slide:** 3 or 4 short sentences at most. Delete anything that isn't needed. High contrast so it's easy to read (readability beats branding here). A Canva font that isn't in Instagram stands out, but avoid hard-to-read script fonts. Use Canva's Instagram Story size (1080 × 1920) and keep text inside the margins.

## Carousels and slideshows

A swipeable post of several slides (portrait, 1080 × 1350, works well). Great for lists, steps and before and after. Keep it to 5 to 7 slides to start.
- **Slide 1:** the hook (a big, readable promise from `hooks.md`).
- **Slides 2 to 6:** one point per slide, few words, in their look. **Slide 2 has to work on its own too:** Instagram can show a carousel again starting from slide 2 to people who didn't swipe the first time, so it can't be just a lead-in.
- **A recap slide before the last one:** the whole thing as a short list. It's the slide people screenshot and send.
- **Last slide:** the call to action ("Save this for later", "Follow for more", or the product or freebie once they're selling).
- **Number the slides** (3/7), so people can see the end is close, and put their **@handle small on every slide:** screenshots travel without them.
You design them in their Canva. Keep them simple.
Kristin's Canva tip: if they have a flat image they'd like to change, **Edit → Magic Layers** can turn it into a design where every piece is editable (if their Canva has it).

## Captions

The caption depends on the post's job. Kristin uses three kinds.

**1. Growth and everyday posts, the shape:**
1. **First line = the hook.** It matches the text on screen. It's what shows before "more": only about the first 125 characters (roughly 2 lines), so the hook has to land inside them. Never a greeting, a hashtag or an emoji first.
2. **The value:** the story, the steps or the tip, in short lines with space between them.
3. **The call to action:** one clear thing to do.
4. **Keywords:** 2 or 3 of their search phrases, used naturally in the sentences.
5. **Hashtags:** **5 at most on Instagram.** Instagram's limit is 5 per post (https://help.instagram.com/351460621611097). Pick 3 to 5 specific ones that match the topic and their buyer, not huge generic tags.

**2. Lead posts (a free gift for a comment): short.** The first line is the call to action ("Comment DINNER and I'll send it to you"), then a keyword or two and the hashtags. That's it: the message does the rest. **One exception:** if the hook could feel like clickbait, add 3 to 5 short lines on why this matters to them (a mini story or the problem it fixes), then the call to action again. **The comment word:** one easy word with no spaces or emoji (DINNER, not "the dinner list"), and they say it out loud in the video too.

**3. Sales posts, Kristin's caption checklist:**
1. **First line:** the call to action, or a hook so curious they tap "more" (not everyone reads to the end, so the call to action goes up top)
2. **The call to action:** comment a word, or follow
3. **Something relatable, a belief shift, or a short real story**
4. **The value**, or "I'll send it to you"
5. **The solution:** the product (or the freebie)
6. **The call to action again**
7. **2 or 3 keywords and up to 5 hashtags**
With a comment-keyword message, 4 to 6 can be one short paragraph. The full sales post is in `stage-sell.md`, section 1.

Write every caption in their tone, at a 4th to 5th grade reading level (`why-people-buy.md`, section 14). Read it out loud: if they wouldn't say it, change it. **No links in captions:** they can't be clicked. The link goes in the bio or the message.

## Text on screen and keywords (being found)

Instagram's search matches what people type to usernames, profile names, bios, captions, hashtags and places (https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works). Captions count; comments don't.

- **Text on screen:** the hook in 12 words or fewer, and the main keyword when it fits naturally ("Easy 15-minute dinners").
- **The caption:** the main keyword in the first lines, plus 1 or 2 supporting phrases.
- **The profile:** keywords in the name field and bio (stage 2).
- **YouTube Shorts:** a title that starts with the main keyword and says the result ("15-Minute Dinner Ideas: 3 Meals for Busy Weeknights"), and a description with the keyword and their links.
- **Use their keyword set** from Research (`why-people-buy.md`, section 15), and their buyer's phrases more than the formal keywords.
- Never stuff keywords. One natural phrase beats five forced ones.

## The sounds-human check (before you show anything they'll post or send)

Run it on every script, caption, comment and message before you show it. Their followers can hear writing that doesn't sound like a person, and on video they have to say it out loud.
- **Read it the way they'd say it.** Short and long sentences mixed, contractions, no line they'd have to practice. No em dashes (`voice.md`).
- **Cut the phrases that sound like a brand or a bot:** "stop scrolling", "in today's video", "follow for more", "tag someone who needs this", "the algorithm loves this", "run, don't walk", "game-changer", "dive into".
- **Rewrite the shapes that sound written:** "It's not just X, it's Y", three words in a row that mean the same thing, one-word questions ("The result?"), lists with an emoji at the start of every line, and words in capitals for shouting.
- **No greeting or lead-in at the start of a video:** it starts at the sentence they'd normally get to at second six.
- **Never invent a number, a result or a client,** even as a placeholder. If a line needs a number they haven't given you, write {your number} and ask them for it (`rules.md`).
