# Stage 8: Turn followers into buyers

Kristin's Sell method: launch posts, the 14-day plan, message replies, sales posts and calls to action.
**When:** after their first 6 weeks of posting (Kristin's rule). Before that, the call to action is follow, save, comment or the freebie.
Kristin: *without sales posts, you have an audience but no income. Don't be afraid to sell.* If it truly helps, sharing it is helping (`why-people-buy.md`, section 12).

---

## 1. Sales posts (about 10% of posts)

From the **Product and proof** pillar, made with the same care as every other post:
- **How it works:** a quick demo, a screen recording, or the printed pages in use
- **A peek inside:** 2 or 3 pages and what each one does for the buyer
- **Who it's for, and who it isn't for**
- **FAQs and doubts:** answer one of their buyer's real objections per post
- **Real results:** only when they exist, shared with permission. Until then, "what you can expect".
- **A clear invitation:** who it's for, what it helps with, what's inside, the price, and where to get it

Use the sales hooks in `hooks.md` and their message bank (Market).

### The sales post, step by step (Kristin's structure)
A sales post is meant to make their buyer *feel* it: the pain of where they are, and a clear picture of life after. Then buy.
1. **A storytelling hook:** a real before and after. Theirs with the product, or a buyer's, shared with permission. *"This guide took me from staring into the fridge at 5 p.m. to dinner on the table in 15 minutes."* Only if it's true.
2. **The struggle and the desire:** exactly where their buyer is now, in their words, and what they want instead.
3. **The transformation, in the caption:** what changed and how, in a few honest lines (Kristin's sales caption checklist, `stage-create.md`, Captions).
4. **Why the product is the answer:** what it does for them that's hard to do alone.
5. **The call to action:** *"Comment GUIDE and I'll send you the link."* The message goes straight to the product, with one friendly follow-up (section 3).

Expect fewer comments than on a lead post. That's normal: the people who comment on a sales post know what they're asking for, so more of them buy.

## 2. The link

The link in their profile goes to the product, or to the freebie's sign-up page if they're building an email list. On Instagram they can also add a **link sticker** to a Story. Etsy sellers use their Share & Save link.
Every sales post says where the link is ("link in my bio", "tap the link in my Story").

## 3. Comment a keyword (message automation)

Kristin's favorite: "Comment PLANNER and I'll send you the link." People comment, and they get the link in their messages automatically.
- **Lead posts** (`stage-create.md`, "The lead post"): the message links to the freebie's sign-up page, so they join the email list. From week 7 it can mention the product in one friendly line.
- **Sales posts** (section 1): the message links straight to the product.
They can start this before week 7: lead posts use it from the start. While comments are few, sending the gift by hand works fine.
- **Tool:** ManyChat works with Instagram and Facebook, and has a free plan with limits. Check the current plan at manychat.com/pricing before you recommend it, and say plainly what the free plan includes.
- **Write the whole flow** (Kristin's 4 steps):
  1. The post or Reel caption that tells people which word to comment, and why.
  2. The first automatic message: warm, like a real person. The gift comes in the first or second message, with nothing to do first (no "follow me to unlock", no questions to answer). That feels like a trick, and people remember it. One easy question can come after the gift.
  3. The message with the link, introducing the product in one natural sentence.
  4. A friendly follow-up 24 hours later for anyone who took the freebie but didn't buy. Once only, never pushy.
- Keep every message short, warm and human. No fake scarcity.

## 4. Replying to messages

When someone replies to a Story or asks a question, keep it simple and personal:
- Thank them, and ask one easy question about what they're working on ("Are you planning dinners for the whole family, or just short on time?").
- If the product fits, share the link in one line, with why it fits *them*.
- If it doesn't fit, say so kindly, and point to something free that helps. A sale that doesn't help them isn't a win.
Write 2 or 3 reply templates for them to personalize. Never copy-paste the same message to everyone.
**They move first.** Message people who commented, answered a Story or wrote to them. Never send the same message to lots of people who didn't, and never use a tool that does: it gets accounts restricted.

**Comments under their own posts,** sorted so the important ones get answered first:
- **The comment word:** send what they asked for (section 3).
- **Someone describing the problem they solve:** answer it fully, in public, then one line offering more help. The public answer is what makes the next person write to them.
- **A question lots of people have:** **reply with a Reel.** On Instagram they can answer a comment with a new Reel; the comment shows on the video and the person who asked gets notified. The hook is already written: it's the question.
- **Kind words** ("love this", 🔥): a like and a few words back.
- **Criticism:** agree with the part that's true first, then hold their ground, once. **Rude or spam:** no reply. Hide abusive comments; that's what the comment controls are for.

**A collab pitch** (a post made with another account in the same space, which shows on both accounts' grids: one of the strongest ways to grow without paying anyone). Before pitching, comment on their posts for a couple of weeks. Then 4 short lines: something specific they've noticed about the other person's account, the idea, what the other person gets, and what they'd need from them. If there's no reply: one follow-up after about 4 days with something new (never "just bumping this"), and a last one around day 10 saying they'll leave it there. Then stop.

## 5. Stories that sell

Once they're selling, **a call to action several times a week** in their Stories: the product, the freebie, or "reply {word}". People chose to tap the Story, so it doesn't feel pushy.
Mix it into a normal Story day: life → making the product → a tip → the call to action.
On a day that's all about the product: about 3 slides of context (the problem, a moment from their day, the story), 1 slide with the offer, and 1 slide of real proof.

## 6. Launching a product (a new product, or their first big push)

### Before launch: a waitlist (Kristin's own DREAM Engine launch)
For a bigger product, or when they want buyers ready on day one, open a free waitlist about 3 weeks before the doors open.
- **The ask:** a comment keyword ("Comment WAITLIST and I'll send you the link"), with their freebie as a thank-you on the page right after they join. The waitlist gets something real: the link first, a launch price, or a bonus. Only what's true (`why-people-buy.md`, section 10).
- **Rotate three kinds of posts, never feature demos only.** Someone can love seeing the product and still not understand why they need it.
  1. **The problem** (Problems pillar, growth): the exact struggle in their buyer's words, and why guessing costs them time. Then one line: that's why they're making it.
  2. **The transformation** (Product pillar, nurture): a real walkthrough of one part. Show what the buyer gets out of it, not just the feature. "From 'I don't know what to make' to three ideas that fit you" beats "here's my idea feature."
  3. **The outcome** (Education pillar): "If I were starting from scratch, here's what I'd want help with first." Walk through the steps a beginner needs, and how the product guides each one.
- **Weave in behind the scenes of making it** (Story pillar): why they're building it, what they're testing, what they changed because of their followers' answers. Watching it come together builds trust before launch.
- **Stories that show who's getting ready to buy:**
  - **A poll:** "What's the hardest part of {their topic}?" with choices that match the product's steps (for a digital product: picking an idea, checking it'll sell, making it, getting sales). Within a day or two, post a short demo that answers the top answer.
  - **The waitlist link,** and what joiners get before launch: the date, their head start, the freebie, sneak peeks.
  - **A question box:** "What would you need to see to feel ready to get this?" Every answer is a worry. Answer it in a post, the sales page FAQ or an email before launch (`sales-calls.md`, section 5).

### The 14-day plan (Kristin's)
- **Days 1 to 7: build trust.** No selling. Helpful, relatable posts about the problem.
- **Days 8 to 11: tease it.** Curiosity and problem-focused posts: "something's coming", behind the scenes of making it.
- **Day 12: launch.** Announce it.
- **Day 14: follow up.** Show how it works or what's inside. A real result only if a real buyer has shared one with permission. Never invented.
For each post: the format, the hook (from `hooks.md`), and one line on what it covers.

### The 3 launch posts
1. **Teaser (2 days before):** the exact problem their buyer feels, and what it's costing them. A hint that something's coming, without naming it. End with a question to get comments.
2. **Launch day:** open with the result, not the product. What's inside. One sentence on what life looks like after using it. Where to get it, and the price.
3. **2 days after:** how it works, or a peek inside, or a real buyer's words (with permission). **Never written "as if a customer got a result."** That's a fake testimonial (`rules.md`, rule 3).
150 to 220 words each, with up to 5 hashtags on Instagram.

### Launch Stories (Kristin's findings)
Tease for about two weeks with behind the scenes (working on it, why it matters). In the last days before launch, post more Stories a day: the problem, the making of it, how it works, and a countdown to the real launch date with a call to action. Don't run launch mode more than every couple of months, or it stops meaning anything.

### Honest urgency only
A real launch price that really ends, a real bonus for the first buyers, a real date. Never fake countdowns, "only 3 left" on a download, or a price that "goes up tonight" when it doesn't (`why-people-buy.md`, section 10).

## 7. Going live to sell (or a sales call)

A live (an Instagram or Facebook Live, or a Skool live call) is a great launch moment, and a sales call fits higher-priced offers like a course, coaching or a membership. Plan it with `sales-calls.md`: section 6 for a live, sections 2 to 5 for a call. It covers the questions to ask, the deck in their Canva, saying the price, kind answers to worries, and a practice run first.
- **Before a live:** tease it in Stories for a few days with the date and time, and a reminder an hour before.
- **After a live:** reply to every comment and message the same day, and keep the replay up (or post a short clip from it) with the link.
