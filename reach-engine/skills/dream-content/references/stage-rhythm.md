# Stage 6: Your rhythm (the 30-day content system)

Kristin's Rhythm method: the posting commitment, weekly calendar, balance check, content filter, batching, scheduling and engagement.
Goal: *a simple system they can repeat every week, so they never start from scratch.*

Kristin's rule: **consistency beats volume.** Posting 3 times a week, every week, beats posting every day for two weeks and then disappearing. Their first 90 days of consistent posting will teach them more than any course.

---

## 1. Pick their rhythm (Kristin's three levels)

From their hours in the notes. Pick ONE and stick to it:

| Level | Time a week | Their week |
|---|---|---|
| **Beginner** | 1 to 2 hours | Reel on Monday · carousel on Thursday · 2 or 3 Stories on Friday |
| **Growing** | 2 to 4 hours | Reel Monday · carousel Tuesday · Stories Wednesday · Reel Friday · Story Saturday |
| **All in** | 5+ hours | A Reel every day · a carousel twice a week · Stories every day |

On YouTube, "Reel" means a Short; on Facebook, the same posts are shared from Instagram.
Recommend the level below what they think they can do. It's easy to overestimate what we'll keep up. They can level up after 30 days. If they truly have the time to post once, twice or three times a day, the rotation card has the mix for each (`stage-pillars-study.md`, "All in").

**The content filter** (before anything gets made): *does this fit one of my 4 pillars, and does it speak to my buyer?* If not, don't post it. A focused, consistent feed builds trust much faster than a random one.

## 2. The weekly system (repeat every week)

| Day | What they do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan day** | Pick this week's posts from their idea bank and real-life list: the right mix of pillars and jobs. You write the content pack. | 15 minutes with you |
| **Batch day** | Film or make everything for the week in one sitting. Kristin's mentor used to record a batch of short videos in an hour. | 30 to 60 minutes |
| **Schedule** | Schedule the posts (in the Instagram app or Meta Business Suite for Instagram and Facebook; YouTube Studio for Shorts). Check the current tools. | 15 minutes |
| **Every day** | 10 to 15 minutes: reply to every comment and message, and leave a few thoughtful comments on bigger accounts in their niche. | 10 to 15 minutes |
| **Story days** | Post their Stories through the day (`stage-create.md`). | a few minutes, a few times |
| **Review day** | The weekly check-in (`stage-grow.md`): what got seen, saved, shared and clicked. | 10 minutes with you |

**Replying matters most when they're small.** Kristin: reply to every comment and message. It's their biggest advantage over big creators, and the people who find them early become their most loyal buyers.
**Commenting on bigger creators' posts** (something real, not "great post!") puts them in front of those creators' followers. How to do it well:
- **Their daily list of about 10 accounts:** 5 bigger accounts their buyer follows (comment early, in the first hour, before there are hundreds of comments), 3 accounts about their size (they tend to comment back), and 2 people who could buy from them (comment for a few weeks before ever messaging, and never pitch). Rotate the list, so it isn't the same 3 accounts every day.
- **A comment that gets noticed does one of these:** adds a real number or their own experience ("Same here: I tried this for 2 weeks and…"), adds the case the post missed, asks the real question the post skipped, or builds on one line from it.
- **1 to 3 sentences, one idea.** Never "Great post", "Love this", "So true", emoji only, or a summary of the video they just watched. Never their link or offer.

## 3. The weekly content pack (you make it)

Every week, on plan day, make their content pack. **Follow their rotation card** (`stage-pillars-study.md`, "Your content rotation"): it says which pillar and which job each post gets this week. Pick the topic and angle from their idea bank and real-life list.

### What to post each day (Kristin's two ways)
1. **Newer accounts (fewer than about 60 posts): a topic + a proven hook.** Take a topic from their big list or idea bank, pick a hook shape from their **Proven hooks and phrases** (their Study collection), and put their topic and keyword into it (`stage-create.md`, "Borrow a proven shape"). New accounts are still testing, so try different topics, formats and phrases, and let the check-ins show what lands.
2. **Established accounts (about 60 posts or more, with some traction): remake a winner.** Look at last month's posts and find the one with the most comments (more comments, more leads, more sales). Then remake it one of two ways:
   - **Same value, new hook:** deliver the same tip or free gift, packaged with a fresh hook.
   - **Same hook and shape, new topic:** keep what made people stop, and use it for a different topic or gift.
   Kristin's findings: something that worked once usually works again, so this becomes the main way to decide once they have the posts to learn from.

For each post:
- the day, pillar and job (from the rotation card)
- the format (Reel or Short, carousel, Story, photo)
- **3 hook options** (`hooks.md`), with their pick saved
- **the text on screen** (12 words or fewer, with a keyword when it fits)
- **the script, shot by shot** (what they say | text on screen | what to show), or the carousel slides, designed in their Canva
- **the caption**, the kind that fits the job (`stage-create.md`, Captions: everyday, lead post or sales post)
- **the Reel checklist**, ticked (`stage-create.md`)
- **the call to action**, matched to the week (no selling in the first 6 weeks)
Plus **their Stories for the week:** which days, a slide plan for each, the poll or question, and any call to action.

**The balance check:** compare the pack with their rotation card. Count the growth, nurture and sales posts, and the posts from each pillar; no pillar twice in a row, and no sales post before week 7. If it's off, fix it before they film.
Save the pack in their notes ("This week's content pack") as made → filmed → posted. Don't make spreadsheets or calendar apps (`rules.md`, rule 10).

## 4. Their first 30 days

A brand new account is inside Kristin's **6 weeks of growth and nurture**, so the first month never sells. It builds the habit, the audience and the trust.

| Week | Focus | What's different this week |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Show up** | Start, imperfectly | Profile live, first 10 followers invited, their first 3 posts (`stage-create.md`), Stories on their Story days. |
| **2. Find your voice** | Keep the rhythm | Follow their rotation card. Try 2 of the formats from stage 4. Their first lead post (a free gift for a comment). A Story poll. |
| **3. Do more of what works** | Repeat what's landing | Reuse the hook shape and format that got the most saves or shares, on a new topic. Add a Story-and-personality post. |
| **4. Look back** | The 30-day review | Their top 3 posts. Kristin's mentor: post for 30 days without changing the format, then do more of the top 3 (`stage-grow.md`). Plan month 2. |

**Weeks 5 and 6:** keep going, and start warming up softly: behind the scenes of their product, "what I'm making", the questions their buyers ask. Still no hard sell.
**From week 7:** selling posts begin (about 60/30/10, `stage-sell.md`).

**The busy-week version:** if a week falls apart, the minimum that still counts is one post and one Story day, plus replying. Done beats perfect.
**Their number:** with them, decide how many posts they'll make before they judge anything (for example 30). Quiet weeks are normal at the start.

## 5. Reuse what they make

Good ideas work everywhere. Only the format changes:
- **Instagram → Facebook:** connected accounts share the same post.
- **Reel → YouTube Short:** the same vertical video, with a keyword title.
- **A popular post → a carousel, a Story series, an email** to their list, or a pin (if they use Pinterest).
- **The best-performing short videos** become ideas for longer videos later (stage 7).
- **One long piece → a week of posts.** A live, a podcast, a YouTube video, a long email or a call recap usually holds 4 to 6 posts. Read the whole thing and pull out what stands on its own: claims people could argue with, numbers, short stories with a moment that cost or won them something, how-it-works explanations, mistakes, and lines that are already quotable. Then pick a format for each: stories, claims and mistakes become Reels (they need their voice and face), steps become carousels (people re-read them), and a quotable line becomes a Story slide. Every post has to make sense on its own (never "as I said in my live"). If it's their own video, use the real clip. If the piece gives fewer than 4, it's thin: say so.
