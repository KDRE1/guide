# Stages 3 and 4: Your pillars and studying what works

Kristin's method for content pillars: the buyer's big list, core topics, the customer journey, content pillars, content types, and turning pillars into ideas.
Goal: *they never wonder what to post again.*

Kristin's chain: **Research** (who is she?) → **The big list** (what does she say and ask, in her words?) → **Core topics** (what am I known for?) → **Customer journey** (what does she need?) → **Content pillars** (how do I talk about it?) → **Content types** (what job does this post do?) → **Content ideas** (what do I actually post?).

**Kristin's two questions** replace "what should I post today?" (a hard question when you start from a blank page every time):
> 1. **What do I want to talk about?** (a pillar)
> 2. **What job do I want this content to do?** (growth, nurture or sales)

And they're not starting from scratch: their research and customer journey already hold the answers. Kristin: *good marketing starts with the person you're trying to help,* not with what you feel like talking about.

If their notes don't have a marketing map (customer journey) yet, draft one first, quickly: the 4 stages (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, ready to buy) with one message each, from their Research. The DREAM Engine's Market step has the full version.

## Stage 3: Your pillars

### Part 1: Your buyer's big list (no gaps)
Kristin: *before anything else, know exactly who you're talking to.* It's the difference between followers and buyers. Their Research already found a lot; this part makes sure nothing is missing, because every topic, hook and caption from here on comes from this list.

**1. The quick check, from their notes.** Show three short lists, each with at least 2 items (5 is better):
- **Struggles:** what's hard for their buyer right now
- **Wants:** the results they wish for, in things they can see or count
- **Questions:** what they ask about getting from where they are to where they want to be (not only what they ask before buying)

Most of it is already in their Research notes. If a list has fewer than 2, fill it with them, one easy question at a time ("What does she ask you most about dinner?"), or with a quick search of real conversations, quoted with links, the way Research does. Never invent them. This short list is usually 6 to 15 items.

**2. Grow it into the big list.** Each item becomes a search, to find more of what their buyer really asks and the exact words they use. Turn an item into something people would type ("easy dinners" → "how to make easy dinners for picky kids").
- **Google's "People also ask" box** shows the questions people search about the same thing. You can't always see that box yourself, so it's a short trip for them: search their top 3 to 5 items on Google, scroll to **People also ask**, and send a screenshot of each. (On free Claude, start with 3.) Search the same items yourself too, and add real questions from pages you open, with the link.
- **The "Short videos" tab** on the same search shows videos that already do well for it. For each search, they open the top 5 (the top 10 for their single most important one) and send a screenshot or a line each: the hook on screen and the first thing said, the topic, and the caption's first line. Any that are on Instagram, they save in Instagram (a saved collection called **Study**) for stage 4.
- From every screenshot, add each question, hook and phrase that's new and fits their buyer, word for word.
- **Listen for the words that repeat:** "quick", "how long", "cheap", "without", "for beginners", "in a week". That's how their buyer talks every day, so their posts should talk that way too.

Kristin: *it takes a little time, and it's worth every minute.* Done well, it fills a page or two. You do all the sorting; they only do the short trips.

**3. Find the patterns.** Kristin's way is to circle the phrases that repeat, one color per theme. Do it for them: group the whole list by theme and by repeated words, and count how often each comes up. Show the top themes, each with its count and 2 or 3 examples, plus the 10 phrases that repeat most.
Save "My big list" (themes with counts) and "Their words" (the repeated phrases) in their notes.

### Part 2: Your 3 or 4 core topics (what you're known for)
Kristin: *you can't solve every problem for everyone.* Most of their posts should come back to the same 3 or 4 core topics, so the right person knows exactly why to follow. An account that talks about everything in its niche (toddler dinners one day, college meal prep the next, then dinners for seniors) speaks to a different person every day and builds trust with no one.
- **Pick them from the big list:** the 3 or 4 themes that came up most and that lead to their product. A theme that came up less but that they love, and that fits the product, can still be one. Their call.
- **This is also how they stand out:** two accounts in the same niche can pick different core topics (one: 15-minute dinners, grocery lists and picky eaters; another: batch cooking, freezer meals and budget shopping).
- **One core topic holds dozens of posts.** Show 6 to 8 quick ideas under each, so they see it won't run dry. Kristin's dinner guide, core topic "15-minute dinners": one-pan dinners, 5-ingredient dinners, pantry dinners, kid-approved dinners, sports-night dinners, no-chop dinners, and more.
- Posting outside the core topics now and then is fine. Most posts stay inside them.

Save "My core topics". The 4 pillars below are the 4 ways they talk about these core topics.

### The 4 content pillars (how you talk about your core topics)
Every post fits one of these. The pillars stay the same for everyone; the topics inside them come from their core topics and big list.

| Pillar | What it sounds like | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Problems and struggles** | "I understand you." | pain points, frustrations, questions, mistakes, obstacles, what isn't working |
| **2. Helpful education** | "Let me help you." | tips, how-to's, simple solutions, mistakes to avoid, answers |
| **3. Story and personality** | "Get to know me." | their story, lessons, process, behind the scenes, wins and struggles, opinions |
| **4. Product and proof** | "Here's how I can help." | the product, what's inside, how it works, demos, FAQs, real results, before and after |

Fill in 4 or 5 topics per pillar from their notes: their core topics and big list (struggles, wants and questions, in their words), the gap and promise from Define, their story from what they've told you (never invented), and the product from Execute. Kristin's rule: *don't ask "what sounds good for me to post?" Ask "based on everything I know about my customer, what does she actually care about?"*

**Kristin's coaching for each pillar** (share the line that fits when you show their topics):
- **Problems and struggles:** people stop when they feel understood. *You don't always need to jump in with the solution. Sometimes the most powerful content simply helps someone recognize and name what they're experiencing.* Her example: the busy mom thinks she's bad at dinner planning, but the real problem is deciding what to cook at 5 o'clock, tired and hungry. The problem isn't that she's failing; the system she's using isn't working for her.
- **Helpful education:** *you don't have to give away your entire product.* Give one helpful step; the product is the complete system. One useful tip is enough: if someone leaves thinking "that was helpful, I can use that," the post did its job. Helpful posts often show people they need the deeper solution.
- **Story and personality:** many people teach similar things, so part of why someone follows is *them*. It's **not** a personal diary (Kristin discourages sharing everything). Share what helps their buyer connect: why they started, a mistake, how they make the product, what they're learning, an opinion about their topic. People connect with people before they buy from them.
- **Product and proof:** people need time to understand an offer. Show someone flipping through it, what's included, a feature, an FAQ, why they made it, how someone would use it, and real feedback. *Never assume your audience knows everything about what you sell. You see your product every day. Your followers don't.*

### The 3 jobs a post can do (content types)
Kristin: *not every post needs to sell.* People are at different points in their journey, so each post has one job:

| Job | What it does | Who it's for (their journey) | What they need from the post |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🌱 **Growth** | Helps new people find them. Relatable, searchable, shareable. ("3 signs…", "5 mistakes…", "If you're struggling with…") | **Unaware** and **problem-aware**: feeling the struggle, maybe without a name for it | **Connection:** "that is exactly me." A relatable moment, or one quick tip. |
| 💚 **Nurture** | Builds trust with people who already know them. Teach, connect, share. | **Problem-aware** and **solution-aware**: they know what's wrong and want help | **Education and clarity:** a small win, how their approach works, behind the scenes. Free content is the first step; the product is the complete system. |
| 💰 **Sales** | Helps ready people take the next step. Show, explain, prove. | **Ready to buy** | **An invitation:** who it's for, the problem it solves, what's inside, the price, proof when it's real, and where to get it. |

**What each job looks like in practice** (Kristin's findings):
- 🌱 **Growth** is made to be **shared**. Every share puts them in front of people who don't follow them yet, so the goal is views, shares and follows. Relatable moments their buyer would send to a friend, trends and memes that fit their buyer, quick how-to's, tips and tricks, and short series ("part 1 of 5").
- 💚 **Nurture** goes **deeper than a quick tip**: it answers a real question from their big list, shifts a belief that holds their buyer back ("I don't have time to plan dinners"), shares an honest opinion, or gives real steps toward the buyer's goal. The goal is trust, and **comments**: the strongest nurture post offers something free for a comment (the lead post, `stage-create.md`). Lead posts with a free gift usually get the most comments of any post, and every comment is a new lead.
- 💰 **Sales** **inspires and shows what's possible**: their real story, a real buyer's words (with permission), a real before and after, the pain point, a doubt answered before it comes up, and the product as the answer (`stage-sell.md`). Sales posts usually get fewer comments than lead posts, but the people who comment are ready to buy.

**Pillar + job = an idea.** Kristin's teaching: *What am I talking about? (the pillar) + What job is this post doing? (the type) = a content idea.* Any pillar can do any job.

**One job per post.** Decide it before making the post. A post that tries to grow, teach everything, tell their life story and sell all at once gets confusing.
- **Growth wants right-fit people, not random views.** A million views from people who'll never care doesn't build a business. Growth posts start with something the buyer already recognizes (a problem, a frustration, a mistake, a wish, a question). Its goal is to *begin the relationship.*
- **Nurture matters most for beginners:** trust rarely comes from one post. It's built through repeated, helpful moments.
- **Sales doesn't have to be pushy.** Kristin: *if you never talk about your offer, you make it harder for the right person to buy from you.* The best sales posts answer the buyer's real questions: *Is this for me? Will this help me? What exactly am I getting? How does it work? Why should I trust this?*

### Their content grid (the first thing you build)
Kristin's 12-box grid: the 4 pillars down the side, the 3 jobs across the top, one idea in each box. Her example, for a 15-minute dinner guide for busy moms:

| Pillar | 🌱 Growth | 💚 Nurture | 💰 Sales |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problems and struggles** | "5 reasons dinner feels so stressful" | "Why I stopped trying to make complicated dinners" | "If dinner stresses you out, here's how my guide can help" |
| **Helpful education** | "3 easy dinners you can make in 15 minutes" | "My 5-minute dinner planning routine" | "Here's exactly how my dinner guide works" |
| **Story and personality** | "The dinner routine that changed my evenings" | "What my family actually eats on busy nights" | "Why I created this guide for busy moms" |
| **Product and proof** | "What I wish I knew about meal planning" | "A peek inside my dinner guide" | "20 easy dinners + grocery lists inside the guide" |

Build **their** grid from their notes and show it on one screen. For a beginner it's the "aha": twelve posts already waiting, and a clear picture of how the same topic can do three different jobs. Their idea bank grows from it.

### Your content rotation (how the pillars and jobs take turns)
Explain it to them in two lines, plainly:
> Every post gets **a pillar** (what you talk about) and **a job** (growth, nurture or sales). We go around your 4 pillars in order, so you never sound repetitive, and each post gets the job your week needs.

**The rotation rules** (you plan with them; they never have to work it out):
1. **The pillars go round in order:** Problems → Education → Story → Product → back to Problems. Never the same pillar twice in a row, and every pillar shows up at least every 2 weeks.
2. **The jobs follow Kristin's mix:**
   - **The first 6 weeks of posting: growth and nurture only.** No selling yet. Entertain and educate, so the right people find them and start to trust them.
   - **After 6 weeks:** about **60% growth, 30% nurture, 10% sales.**
3. **Every pillar gets every job over time.** The Product pillar can be growth ("What I wish I knew…") or nurture ("A peek inside…") long before it ever sells.
4. **The sales post moves around.** One month it's from Product ("what's inside"), the next from Problems ("if this stresses you out…"), then from Story ("why I made this"). Selling never sounds the same twice.
5. **Topics come back with a new angle** (the angles below), so a good topic can be used again and again.
6. **It's not every combination every week.** The grid shows options, not homework.

**Kristin's self-check,** for whenever they're stuck or posting on their own (teach them this one):
> - **What pillar haven't I talked about recently?**
> - **What does my audience need right now?**
> - **Do I want to attract someone new, build trust, or help someone understand my offer?**

**Their rotation card**, by their rhythm level (`stage-rhythm.md`). Each 4-week card repeats with new topics:

**Beginner** (Reel Monday, carousel Thursday, Stories Friday)

| | Weeks 1 to 6 (growth and nurture) | From week 7 (about 60/30/10) |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Mon: Problems · growth · Thu: Education · nurture | Mon: Problems · growth · Thu: Education · growth |
| **Week 2** | Mon: Story · growth · Thu: Product · nurture | Mon: Story · nurture · Thu: Product · growth |
| **Week 3** | Mon: Problems · nurture · Thu: Education · growth | Mon: Problems · growth · Thu: Education · nurture |
| **Week 4** | Mon: Story · nurture · Thu: Product · growth | Mon: Story · growth · Thu: Product · **sales** |

Weeks 5 and 6 repeat weeks 1 and 2 with new topics. From week 7, each month has 5 growth, 2 nurture and 1 sales post; next month the sales post moves to another pillar.

**Growing** (Reel Monday, carousel Tuesday, Reel Friday, Stories Wednesday and Saturday)

| | Weeks 1 to 6 (growth and nurture) | From week 7 (about 60/30/10) |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Problems · growth · Education · growth · Story · nurture | Problems · growth · Education · growth · Story · nurture |
| **Week 2** | Product · growth · Problems · growth · Education · nurture | Product · growth · Problems · nurture · Education · growth |
| **Week 3** | Story · growth · Product · growth · Problems · nurture | Story · growth · Product · nurture · Problems · growth |
| **Week 4** | Education · growth · Story · growth · Product · nurture | Education · nurture · Story · growth · Product · **sales** |

(Monday · Tuesday · Friday, in that order.) From week 7, each month has 7 growth, 4 nurture and 1 sales post.

**All in** (a Reel every day, 2 carousels, Stories every day): go around the pillars in order with every post. Weeks 1 to 6: 2 growth posts for every nurture post. From week 7: each week about 5 growth, 3 nurture and 1 sales.
- **Posting once a day:** growth, growth, nurture, and repeat (Monday growth, Tuesday growth, Wednesday nurture, and so on).
- **Twice a day** (only if it truly fits their life): one growth and one nurture each day.
- **Three times a day** (rare, and only for a while): two growth and one nurture each day. It can speed up a new account, but more posts never beat a rhythm they can keep.

**Stories follow along lightly:** each Story day touches that day's pillar (a relatable moment, a quick tip, everyday life, making the product). Until week 7, Story calls to action are a reply, a poll or the freebie. After that, Stories carry most of the gentle selling: a call to action several times a week (`stage-sell.md`).

Save their rotation card in their notes, with the date they start posting and the date selling posts begin (6 weeks later).

### Turning pillars into ideas
Kristin: think of the pillars as **big buckets**, with topics inside each one. A topic ("meal planning") isn't a post yet; it's a starting point. The **angle** is simply *what specifically am I going to say about this topic?*
Kristin's formula: **pillar + topic + angle = a content idea.** One topic is never just one post. Angles to reuse:
- **Teach:** "How to…"
- **Mistake:** "Stop doing…"
- **Problem:** "If you're struggling with…"
- **Tip:** "Try this…"
- **Question:** "Have you ever wondered…?"
- **Story:** "What happened when…"
- **Opinion:** "I don't believe…"
- **List:** "5 things…"
- **Before and after**, **step by step**, **beginner advice**, **myth**

Kristin's example, the topic "meal planning": three meal-planning mistakes that made my week harder · one thing I do before grocery shopping that saves me time every week · my 10-minute Sunday planning routine · why I started planning dinners · what's inside the planning section of my guide.
**They can, and should, talk about the same topic again.** Kristin: *your audience doesn't need you to constantly invent new topics. They need you to consistently talk about the things they care about.* The trick is just not saying the same thing twice: change the angle. And skip any angle that doesn't fit (no real story for that topic? skip the story angle). We never force content.

**Same topic, different angle, different format, different job.** They don't need a new topic every day. Kristin: *you want to be known for certain conversations.* Her example, the topic "meal planning": a meal-planning mistake → their personal meal-planning routine → why they started meal planning → the meal-planning section inside their product. Four posts, one topic, and each can be a different format (a Reel, a carousel, a Story, an email).

**When they're stuck, Kristin's steps:** choose a pillar → choose a topic → choose an angle → choose the job → create. Her walk-through: Helpful education → easy dinners → a quick tip → nurture → *"My easiest trick for getting dinner on the table faster."* No waiting for a brilliant idea; just the steps.
**Simple does not mean boring.** One small tip that solves one small problem is enough. Help one person with one thing. A post doesn't have to go viral or teach everything they know.

### Their content idea bank
Kristin's idea bank is simply a list to come back to: the topic, the angle, the idea, and the job. Whenever an idea comes to them, they save it (in their business notes, or the Notes app on their phone), so they never start from scratch.
Build their first one for them: **5 ideas per pillar (20 ideas)**. For each one:
- the pillar and the job (growth, nurture or sales)
- the hook, from `hooks.md`
- the best format (Reel or Short, carousel, Story, photo)
- a simple call to action

Then check it with Kristin's four questions and drop anything that fails: *Would my buyer care? Is it specific? Does it connect to my business? Would I actually make it?*
Show it to them as a draft to react to, never as final. Kristin: use AI to speed up the thinking, but the final strategy has to feel like *them*. Never produce a giant generic list ("100 content ideas"); build every idea from their buyer, their topics and their words. AI's ideas are *suggestions, not instructions*: keep what they'd actually say and what leads back to their offer, make anything generic more specific, and drop the rest.
**Structure, not a box:** they can still jump on a trend or share something spontaneous, as long as it fits a pillar and speaks to their buyer.

### Their real-life idea list (every week)
Kristin's mentor: the best ideas come from real life, not from guessing or AI. Each week, ask them for a few lines on:
- a question a buyer or follower asked
- a mistake they made, and what they learned
- something they used to believe that turned out wrong
- a small win (theirs, or a buyer's, shared with permission)
- what they're working on right now
Turn each into an idea with a pillar and a job. These always sound like them.

## Stage 4: Study what works

Kristin's rule: don't guess what works in their niche; look at what's already working. Kristin: *do this before making content.* It's what makes their posts reach the right people (search) and get them to comment.

**Start with their own best posts** (only if they already have a business page with about 60 posts or more, stage 1 answer c): which posts from the last month got the most comments, and what was the topic, the hook and the free gift? Those are proven with *their* audience. On Instagram with Apify connected, pull them yourself (`apify.md`, "The 30-day look"); otherwise ask for a screenshot of their grid with the comment counts.

1. **Find 3 or 4 big accounts** in their niche, on their platform: about 50,000 followers or more, accounts their buyer already follows and they look up to. Search their keywords and their words (from the big list).
   - **Add 1 or 2 accounts closer to their size** (up to about 10 times their followers). A format that works at 2 million followers often works *because* of the 2 million.
   - **Add 1 or 2 accounts in a different niche with the same buyer** (a mom-life account, when they sell dinner planners). New formats often show up there first. Borrow the format only.
2. **Pick each account's 2 or 3 best posts from the last month, by comments first.** Kristin: *comments are people raising their hand.* More comments means more leads, and more leads means more sales. Then views. (Shares and saves matter too, but only the account owner can see those.)
   - **Instagram, with Apify connected:** pull the posts yourself (`apify.md`, "Studying accounts"). If Apify isn't connected yet, offer it once (`apify.md`, "Connect it").
   - **Otherwise:** ask for one screenshot of each account's grid or Reels tab.
   - **Then check each post against that account's usual.** Big numbers can fool you: 400,000 views is a normal day for an account with 2 million followers, but for an account with 4,000 followers it means that post found something. The account's usual is the middle view count of its last 12 Reels. A post at about 3 times its usual or more is a real winner worth studying; one under about 1.5 times is just a normal day, however big the number looks. With Apify, work it out yourself from the posts you pulled; otherwise estimate it from their Reels tab.
3. **For each one, note:**
   - what it was (a Reel, a carousel, a trend; if it uses a trending sound, they can save the audio in the app)
   - the hook (on screen and spoken)
   - how the value was delivered: taught in the video, a list in the caption, or a free gift sent to everyone who commented a word
   - the topic: what were they teaching or talking about?
   - the exact keywords and phrases in the hook and the caption
4. **Search their top phrases.** They type 2 or 3 of the phrases from "Their words" into Instagram search and open the **Reels** tab: the top results are what does best for that phrase. They save 5 to 10 of them and send a screenshot of the results; note the hooks and the words that keep showing up.
5. **Save everything** in their Instagram **Study** collection (with the Short videos from the big list). It's their hook bank for every week.
6. **Turn the patterns into their formats.** Keep the *shape*, never the words. A Reel that's well above its account's usual is worth remaking right away (`apify.md`, "Remake a viral Reel"). "Hook on screen for 3 seconds, then a fast list" can become their own format with their topic and their words.

**Never copy** a creator's content, words or footage. The goal is the pattern, made theirs.

Save "Accounts I study" with the 2 or 3 formats they'll try first, and **"Proven hooks and phrases"**: the hook shapes that keep working (with blanks, like "This {thing} is helping me {result}") and the phrases that keep showing up. These go straight into their first posts (`stage-create.md`) and every week's content pack (`stage-rhythm.md`).
