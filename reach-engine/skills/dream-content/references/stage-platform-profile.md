# Stages 1 and 2: Your platform and your profile

Kristin's method for setting up a platform and profile that grows from zero, or for improving the page they already have.
Goal: *one platform, set up so the right person decides in about 5 seconds to follow.*

---

## Stage 1: Your platform

### Start with the mindset (two or three lines, not a lecture)
- **You don't need an audience to start. You need to start to get an audience.** Everyone starts at zero.
- **Ready comes from starting,** not before it.
- **The goal is not to go viral.** It's to show up for one specific person until they trust you enough to buy.

### First question: started already, or starting from scratch?
Open stage 1 with this one question, so everything after it fits where they really are:
> **Have you already started a page for your business, or are you starting from scratch?**
> a) **Starting from scratch:** no business page yet
> b) **I have a personal account** I could use (family photos, a bit of everything)
> c) **I already have a business page** (which platform, and about how many posts?)
> d) **I'm not sure.** Let's talk it through.

- **a) From scratch:** pick one platform (below), then the handle comes first in stage 2.
- **b) A personal account:** pick the platform, then "Fresh account, or the one they have?" below. Most people in b) start fresh.
- **c) A business page:** look at it before anything else (connect Apify and run a profile check, `apify.md`, or ask for 2 phone screenshots: the top of the profile and the grid). Then decide together whether to keep and improve it. Save roughly how many posts it has: **with about 60 posts or more,** their own best posts become a second source of ideas later (stage 4, "Start with their own best posts", and `stage-rhythm.md`, "What to post each day").
- **d) Not sure:** one easy question at a time (do you have any account you'd want to use for this?), then the matching path.

Save it as "Started already or from scratch".

### Pick ONE platform
Kristin's rule: *go all in on one. Don't spread yourself thin.* Recommend one from their notes (where their buyer is, their product, their hours, their comfort on camera). If they already have a business page with real followers (answer c), that platform usually wins:

| Platform | Best when | Kristin's take |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** (shared to Facebook) | Their buyer is visual or younger, and scrolls lifestyle, tips and product content | Reels, Stories and tutorials. Best for a personal brand selling digital products. |
| **Facebook** | Their buyer is a bit older, or in parenting, health, business or local life, or they want a free group later | Same posts as Instagram once the two are connected. |
| **YouTube** (Shorts first) | Their product is something people search how-to's for, and they like teaching | Search-driven and long-lasting. Start with Shorts; long videos come later (stage 7). |

Not sure? **Start with the one they already use personally.** It'll feel natural, so they'll keep going.
Instagram and Facebook are both Meta, so once they're connected, one post can go to both.

### Fresh account, or the one they have?
- **Start fresh** if their current account is personal (family photos, random posts), their followers aren't their buyers, or they want a clean, focused start. Decide the handle first (stage 2).
- **Keep and improve it** if they already have an engaged audience in their niche, even a small one. Improving beats starting from zero.
- **Not sure?** Look at the account first, so the choice is based on what's there: connect Apify now and run a profile check (`apify.md`). If they'd rather not connect it, ask for 2 phone screenshots: the top of their profile, and their grid.

### Face or faceless?
- **A face is recommended:** people buy from people, and seeing someone's face builds trust fast.
- **Faceless works too:** screen recordings of the product, hands working in Canva, the printed planner on a kitchen table, text on screen with a voice-over. Save their choice; every script will follow it.
- **Faceless means their own footage, never stock clip packs.** Kristin: the ready-made faceless video templates and clip banks are used by thousands of accounts, so posts made from them blend right in. Film their own B-roll instead: their hands, their desk, their kitchen, their product in use.

Save: started already or from scratch, their platform (and what it's shared to), fresh or existing, face or faceless.

## Stage 2: Your profile

Someone lands on their profile and decides in about 5 seconds. Kristin's mentor calls it the **3-second test**: a glance should tell them who this helps and what to do next.
Build all of it from their notes, show it together, and let them approve. They type it into the app themselves.

### Account type
- **Instagram:** a **professional account** (Creator is right for most digital product sellers). It adds stats, contact buttons and scheduling. Settings → account type and tools → switch to professional account (check the current menu names).
- **Facebook:** a **Page**, not their personal profile, with a category like Digital Creator, Entrepreneur or Education.
- **YouTube:** a channel with their brand name and a handle.

### Handle and name (Kristin's 4 formulas)
Give 2 options for each, then recommend one:
1. **Name + niche keyword:** personal and searchable (@sarahsellsdigital)
2. **Keyword only:** easy to find, great for faceless (@digitalproducttips)
3. **Name + brand word:** personality (@thesarahmethod)
4. **Initials + niche:** short and clean (@kgmarketing)

**The name field** (the bold line under the photo) is searchable. If their name is in the handle, use the name field for keywords ("Canva Templates | Digital Products"). If not, combine them ("Sarah | Canva Templates"). Kristin keeps it under 30 characters.
Instagram's own search guide says it matches searches to usernames, profile names, bios, captions, hashtags and places, and recommends keywords in the name and bio: https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works
Use the same name everywhere they show up (shop, profile, email).

### Bio (Instagram: 150 characters, 3 lines)
- **Line 1, who they help:** an "I help" line about one result their buyer wants.
- **Line 2, why trust them:** a real credential, experience or detail. Never invented. If they have none yet, use what they make ("Simple printable planners for busy moms").
- **Line 3, the call to action:** what to do, pointing down to the link ("⬇ Grab the free dinner list").
Write 5 options in different tones (warm, bold, simple, fun, expert), using 3 of their keywords naturally.

**Facebook About** (4 sentences): who they help → what they offer → why follow → the call to action and link.
**YouTube description:** "I help {who} {result}", plus their product link and their freebie link. Banner: a promise in 3 words or fewer (for example "Dinner, Done Fast"), designed in Canva.

### The link
Their shop or product link, or their freebie's sign-up page if they have an email list (the DREAM Engine's Market step). Etsy sellers use their Share & Save link.
Instagram lets them add a few links. Keep the first one the one that matters.

### Profile photo
Face recommended: clear, well lit, a close-up, a plain background, smiling, facing a window or a ring light. The same photo everywhere.
Faceless: a clean logo or brand graphic, made in Canva in their look.

### Instagram extras
- **Settings that help them be found** (Kristin's picks; names change, so search Settings if they look different): show account suggestions on their profile (on), story replies from everyone (on).
- **Highlights** (the circles under the bio): 6 covers designed in Canva (1080 × 1920) in their colors. Kristin's ideas: About me, My products, Free resource, FAQs, How it works, Reviews (only once real reviews exist). They need at least one Story posted before they can make a highlight.
- **Pinned posts** (up to 3, the first thing a visitor sees):
  1. their main offer: what they sell and who it's for
  2. proof, only when it's real (a buyer's message shared with permission). Until then: how it works, or a peek inside.
  3. their best quick-win tip, so people want to follow
  Until they've posted enough, pin what they have and swap later.

### Facebook extras
- Messenger on, so people can message the Page.
- Turn off tabs they don't use.
- **Featured:** their product, their freebie, or a short intro video. Design a 1200 × 628 graphic in Canva.
- **Connect Instagram and Facebook** (through Meta's Accounts Center or Meta Business Suite) so posts can go to both.

### Their first 10 followers
Kristin's mentor: invite a handful of friends and family when the account starts, even if it's only 10 people. Share it once in groups they're already part of, where the rules allow. Never buy followers or join "follow for follow" groups: those followers will never buy.

### Your look and your thing (how you stand out)
Lots of people post about the same topics. Kristin: *people buy from people they like and feel drawn to,* so the way to stand out is to be more *them*, not more like everyone else.
- **Copying a creator move for move** (the same video, the same words, the same style) is the most common mistake. Big accounts are for inspiration only (stage 4).
- **Their personality:** ask for 3 words that describe how they come across (warm, funny, calm, straight-talking, a little silly, shy but kind). Every script follows those words. Lean into it; don't sand it off.
- **Their look, the same every time:** 1 or 2 fonts and 2 or 3 brand colors on every Reel, carousel, Story and cover. Take them from their shop look in their notes when they have one, so their posts and their product match. Kristin: a feed where every post has a different font and color looks messy and is harder to trust. A consistent one is recognized in a second ("oh, that's her").
- **Their thing:** a small signature that shows up often in the beginning, so people recognize them. A favorite mug in every video, the same corner of their kitchen, a phrase they always say, a color they always wear. Offer 2 or 3 ideas that fit their life; they pick or skip.

Save "My look": fonts | colors | 3 personality words | my thing.

## Checkpoint: your profile

> **Here's your profile:**
> **Platform:** {platform}, shared to {…}
> **Handle:** {handle} · **Name:** {name field}
> **Bio:** {the 3 lines}
> **Link:** {link}
> **Highlights:** {list} · **Pinned:** {plan}
> **Your look:** {fonts} · {colors} · {3 personality words} · {their thing}
>
> Set it up like this? Say **yes**, or tell me what to change.

On yes: save it in their notes, then walk them through typing it in, 3 to 5 steps per message. They tell you when it's live.

## Then: connect Apify and check the live profile

Once it's live, it's time for the Apify step (`apify.md`, "Connect it"), if it isn't connected yet. One message: what it does for them, what it costs (free to start), and the 6 steps.
Then run the profile check (`apify.md`, "The profile check") on their live profile, plus one screenshot of the top of it on their phone. A new account with no posts yet gets the short version: the profile parts only.
Fix what it finds, with them, and save it under **Profile checks**. Then stage 2 is done: celebrate, show the roadmap, and hand off to stage 3.
If they'd rather not connect Apify, check the live profile from that one screenshot instead.
