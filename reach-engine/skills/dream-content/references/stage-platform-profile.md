# Stages 1 and 2: Your platform and your profile

Kristin's method for setting up a platform and profile that grows from zero, or for improving the page they already have.
Goal: *one platform, set up so the right person decides in about 5 seconds to follow.*

---

## Stage 1: Your platform

### Start with the mindset (two or three lines, not a lecture)
- **You don't need an audience to start. You need to start to get an audience.** Everyone starts at zero.
- **Ready comes from starting,** not before it.
- **The goal is not to go viral.** It's to show up for one specific person until they trust you enough to buy.

### First question: started already, or starting from scratch?
Open stage 1 with this one question, so everything after it fits where they really are:
> **Have you already started a page for your business, or are you starting from scratch?**
> a) **Starting from scratch:** no business page yet
> b) **I have a personal account** I could use (family photos, a bit of everything)
> c) **I already have a business page** (which platform, and about how many posts?)
> d) **I'm not sure.** Let's talk it through.

- **a) From scratch:** pick one platform (below), then the handle comes first in stage 2.
- **b) A personal account:** pick the platform, then "Fresh account, or the one they have?" below. Most people in b) start fresh.
- **c) A business page:** look at it before anything else (connect Apify and run a profile check, `apify.md`, or ask for 2 phone screenshots: the top of the profile and the grid). Then decide together whether to keep and improve it. Save roughly how many posts it has: **with about 60 posts or more,** their own best posts become a second source of ideas later (stage 4, "Start with their own best posts", and `stage-rhythm.md`, "What to post each day").
- **d) Not sure:** one easy question at a time (do you have any account you'd want to use for this?), then the matching path.

Save it as "Started already or from scratch".

### Pick ONE platform
Kristin's rule: *go all in on one. Don't spread yourself thin.* Recommend one from their notes (where their buyer is, their product, their hours, their comfort on camera). If they already have a business page with real followers (answer c), that platform usually wins:

| Platform | Best when | Kristin's take |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** (shared to Facebook) | Their buyer is visual or younger, and scrolls lifestyle, tips and product content | Reels, Stories and tutorials. Best for a personal brand selling digital products. |
| **Facebook** | Their buyer is a bit older, or in parenting, health, business or local life, or they want a free group later | Same posts as Instagram once the two are connected. |
| **YouTube** (Shorts first) | Their product is something people search how-to's for, and they like teaching | Search-driven and long-lasting. Start with Shorts; long videos come later (stage 7). |

Not sure? **Start with the one they already use personally.** It'll feel natural, so they'll keep going.
Instagram and Facebook are both Meta, so once they're connected, one post can go to both. They connect them right after their first account is live (stage 2, "Then: connect Instagram and Facebook").

### Fresh account, or the one they have?
- **Start fresh** if their current account is personal (family photos, random posts), their followers aren't their buyers, or they want a clean, focused start. Decide the handle first (stage 2).
- **Keep and improve it** if they already have an engaged audience in their niche, even a small one. Improving beats starting from zero.
- **Not sure?** Look at the account first, so the choice is based on what's there: connect Apify now and run a profile check (`apify.md`). If they'd rather not connect it, ask for 2 phone screenshots: the top of their profile, and their grid.

### Face or faceless?
- **A face is recommended:** people buy from people, and seeing someone's face builds trust fast.
- **Faceless works too:** screen recordings of the product, hands working in Canva, the printed planner on a kitchen table, text on screen with a voice-over. Save their choice; every script will follow it.
- **Faceless means their own footage, never stock clip packs.** Kristin: the ready-made faceless video templates and clip banks are used by thousands of accounts, so posts made from them blend right in. Film their own B-roll instead: their hands, their desk, their kitchen, their product in use.

Save: started already or from scratch, their platform (and what it's shared to), fresh or existing, face or faceless.

## Stage 2: Your profile

Someone lands on their profile and decides in about 5 seconds. Kristin calls it the **3-second test**: a glance should tell them who this helps and what to do next.
Build all of it from their notes, show it together, and let them approve. They type it into the app themselves.

### Account type
- **Instagram:** a **professional account** (Creator is right for most digital product sellers). It adds stats, contact buttons and scheduling. Settings → account type and tools → switch to professional account (check the current menu names).
- **Facebook:** a **Page**, not their personal profile, with a category like Digital Creator, Entrepreneur or Education.
- **YouTube:** a channel with their brand name and a handle.

### Handle and name

**A brand new account? Match it to their shop first** (Kristin, Nov 1). Buyers should see the same name on their shop and on their social media, so they know right away it's the same person. A matching name also makes them easier to find and easier to trust.
1. **Their shop name:** "My brand / shop name" from their DREAM notes, like their Etsy shop or Stan store name. Not in their notes? Ask: "What's your shop called?"
2. **Handle:** exactly their shop name when it's free, written the same way (EasyDinnerMama becomes @easydinnermama). They type it into the handle box on Instagram, which shows whether it's taken. Use the same handle on Facebook and YouTube.
3. **Taken?** Use the closest version that still reads as their shop: add one word a buyer would expect, like shop, studio, co, designs or printables (@easydinnermamashop). Skip random numbers and extra underscores: they're hard to say and can look like spam.
4. **The name field:** their shop name plus a keyword, under 30 characters ("Easy Dinner Mama | Meal Planners").
5. **Facebook Page name:** their shop name too, with the same profile photo or logo as their shop icon.
6. **Save it** in their notes: "Handle | name field", and that it matches their shop.

Would they rather use a different name? That's their call. Say once why matching helps, then go with their choice.
**Keeping an account they already have,** and its handle doesn't match their shop? Offer once to change the handle so it matches. Their followers stay; old links that use the old handle may stop working.
**No shop name yet?** Use Kristin's 4 formulas below, and then use that same name for their shop.

**Kristin's 4 formulas** (when there's no shop name to match). Give 2 options for each, then recommend one:
1. **Name + niche keyword:** personal and searchable (@sarahsellsdigital)
2. **Keyword only:** easy to find, great for faceless (@digitalproducttips)
3. **Name + brand word:** personality (@thesarahmethod)
4. **Initials + niche:** short and clean (@smtemplates)

**The name field** (the bold line under the photo) is searchable. If their name is in the handle, use the name field for keywords ("Canva Templates | Digital Products"). If not, combine them ("Sarah | Canva Templates"). Kristin keeps it under 30 characters.
Instagram's own search guide says it matches searches to usernames, profile names, bios, captions, hashtags and places, and recommends keywords in the name and bio: https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works
Use the same name everywhere they show up (shop, profile, email).

### Bio (Instagram: 150 characters, 3 lines)
- **Line 1, who they help:** an "I help" line about one result their buyer wants.
- **Line 2, why trust them:** a real credential, experience or detail. Never invented. If they have none yet, use what they make ("Simple printable planners for busy moms").
- **Line 3, the call to action:** what to do, pointing down to the link ("⬇ Grab the free dinner list").
Write 5 options in different tones (warm, bold, simple, fun, expert), using 3 of their keywords naturally.

**Facebook About** (4 sentences): who they help → what they offer → why follow → the call to action and link.
**YouTube description:** "I help {who} {result}", plus their product link and their freebie link. Banner: a promise in 3 words or fewer (for example "Dinner, Done Fast"), designed in Canva.

### The link
Their shop or product link, or their freebie's sign-up page if they have an email list (the DREAM Engine's Market step). Etsy sellers use their Share & Save link.
Instagram lets them add a few links. Keep the first one the one that matters.

### Put their shop link in their bio (step by step, never skipped)
The shop they made with the DREAM Engine is where followers become buyers, so their shop link always goes in their bio (Kristin, Nov 1). Do it right after the profile is typed in (Checkpoint, below).
1. **Get the link.** Use **Shop link** from their DREAM notes (Apply). Etsy sellers use their shop's Share & Save link, which earns them 4% back on orders from links they share. If their notes don't have a link, ask them to paste it.
2. **Check that it opens** their shop or product page before it goes in. Open it yourself if you can; otherwise ask them to tap it once.
3. **Walk them through it,** in one message, then wait for "done":
   > **Let's put your shop link in your bio,** so anyone who loves your posts can buy in one tap:
   > 1. In the Instagram app, go to your profile and tap **Edit profile**.
   > 2. Tap **Links**, then **Add external link**.
   > 3. Paste your link: {their link}
   > 4. For the title, type: **{a short title, like "Shop my planners"}**
   > 5. Tap **Done**, then go back to your profile.
   >
   > Now tap the link on your profile. Does it open your shop? Tell me **done**, or what you see.
   - **Facebook Page:** the link goes in the Page's details, as its website or a link (on the Page, **Edit details**). **YouTube:** in YouTube Studio, under **Customization**, add it to the channel's links.
   - Screens change. Check the platform's own help page before you name a button, and if what they see doesn't match, ask what they see.
4. **Which link comes first:** their shop, unless they have a freebie sign-up page. In that case the freebie is first and the shop is second. Line 3 of the bio points down to the first link.
   - **Several links?** If they have more products, a freebie, a YouTube channel and so on, mention once that they can set up a **link in bio page**: one simple page with all their links, and that one link goes in their bio (Kristin, Nov 1).
     - **If they sell on Stan Store or Beacons,** their store page already works as one. They add the extra links there and use their store link in their bio.
     - **Otherwise,** they can use a free link in bio page (Linktree and Beacons both have free plans; check the current plans before you say so), or a page on Systeme.io if they already use it.
     - Use the same order as above, with short titles, in their look (their fonts and colors). If they want it, walk them through it step by step (check the tool's help page first).
     - Then the bio link points to that page, and "Link in bio" in their notes saves both links: the page and the shop.
5. **Save it** in their notes: **Link in bio:** {link}, with the date. Every profile check tests that it still opens (`apify.md`).

### Profile photo
Face recommended: clear, well lit, a close-up, a plain background, smiling, facing a window or a ring light. The same photo everywhere.
Faceless: a clean logo or brand graphic, made in Canva in their look.

### Instagram extras
- **Settings that help them be found** (Kristin's picks; names change, so search Settings if they look different): show account suggestions on their profile (on), story replies from everyone (on).
- **Highlights** (the circles under the bio): 6 covers designed in Canva (1080 × 1920) in their colors. Kristin's ideas: About me, My products, Free resource, FAQs, How it works, Reviews (only once real reviews exist). They need at least one Story posted before they can make a highlight.
- **Pinned posts** (up to 3, the first thing a visitor sees):
  1. their main offer: what they sell and who it's for
  2. proof, only when it's real (a buyer's message shared with permission). Until then: how it works, or a peek inside.
  3. their best quick-win tip, so people want to follow
  Until they've posted enough, pin what they have and swap later.

### Facebook extras
- Messenger on, so people can message the Page.
- Turn off tabs they don't use.
- **Featured:** their product, their freebie, or a short intro video. Design a 1200 × 628 graphic in Canva.
- Connecting Instagram and Facebook comes right after the profile is live ("Then: connect Instagram and Facebook", below).

### Their first 10 followers
Kristin: invite a handful of friends and family when the account starts, even if it's only 10 people. Share it once in groups they're already part of, where the rules allow. Never buy followers or join "follow for follow" groups: those followers will never buy.

### Your look and your thing (how you stand out)
Lots of people post about the same topics. Kristin: *people buy from people they like and feel drawn to,* so the way to stand out is to be more *them*, not more like everyone else.
- **Copying a creator move for move** (the same video, the same words, the same style) is the most common mistake. Big accounts are for inspiration only (stage 4).
- **Ask the personality words and their thing in one message,** together with anything else stage 2 needs from them, each with your pick from what they've told you, so one answer covers it all (`rules.md`, rule 20).
- **Their personality:** ask for 3 words that describe how they come across (warm, funny, calm, straight-talking, a little silly, shy but kind). Every script follows those words. Lean into it; don't sand it off.
- **Their look, the same every time:** 1 or 2 fonts and 2 or 3 brand colors on every Reel, carousel, Story and cover. Take them from their shop look in their notes when they have one, so their posts and their product match. Kristin: a feed where every post has a different font and color looks messy and is harder to trust. A consistent one is recognized in a second ("oh, that's her").
- **Their thing:** a small signature that shows up often in the beginning, so people recognize them. A favorite mug in every video, the same corner of their kitchen, a phrase they always say, a color they always wear. Offer 2 or 3 ideas that fit their life; they pick or skip.

Save "My look": fonts | colors | 3 personality words | my thing.

## Checkpoint: your profile

> **Here's your profile:**
> **Platform:** {platform}, shared to {…}
> **Handle:** {handle} · **Name:** {name field}
> **Bio:** {the 3 lines}
> **Link:** {link}
> **Highlights:** {list} · **Pinned:** {plan}
> **Your look:** {fonts} · {colors} · {3 personality words} · {their thing}
>
> Set it up like this? Say **yes**, or tell me what to change.

On yes: save it in their notes, then walk them through typing it in, 3 to 5 steps per message. Then do "Put their shop link in their bio", above. They tell you when it's live.

## Then: connect Instagram and Facebook (two platforms, one post)

**Only for Instagram or Facebook** (skip this for YouTube). Kristin (Oct 28): once their first account is open and their profile is live, connect it to the other one. They put their effort into one platform, and every post shows up on both. Don't bring it up before their first account is live, and don't mix it into the profile setup.

Say it in one short message, then ask:

> Your {platform} is live! Here's an easy win: let's connect it to {the other one}. You'll put your effort into one, and every post shows up on both, so you get two platforms for the work of one. It takes about 10 minutes. Want to do it now?

- **Yes:** walk them through the path below that matches where they started, 3 to 5 steps per message, and wait for "done" after each part. Menus move around, so open the help page linked to each step before you name a button, and if their screen looks different, ask what they see.
- **Not now:** that's fine. Save it as "not yet", and offer it once more when you build their 30-day system (stage 6).

### Started on Instagram: make a Facebook Page and connect it
1. **Their Instagram is a professional account** (stage 2 set it up). It has to be, to connect to a Page, and it stays public. Help: https://help.instagram.com/502981923235522
2. **Make their Facebook Page**, if they don't have one, on a computer: in Facebook, **Pages** (on the left) → **Create page** → **Public page** → **Next** → **Get started**. Use the same name as their Instagram, a category like Digital creator, Entrepreneur or Education, and their Instagram bio, then **Create Page**. Add the same profile photo and a cover in their look (design it in their Canva). WhatsApp and inviting friends can be skipped. Help: https://www.facebook.com/help/104002523024878
   - They need a personal Facebook account to make a Page, but the Page is separate: family posts stay on their personal profile.
3. **Connect the two**, from the Page on a computer: their profile photo (top right) → **See all profiles** → their Page → the Page's profile photo → **Settings & privacy** → **Settings** → **Linked accounts** (under Permissions) → **Instagram** → **Connect account**, then log in to Instagram when it asks. Help: https://www.facebook.com/help/1148909221857370
4. **Turn on sharing**, in the Instagram app on their phone: their profile → the menu (top right) → **Accounts Center** → **Connected experiences** → **Sharing across profiles**. Share from their Instagram to their Facebook Page, and turn on **Your Instagram posts** (plus Reels and Stories, if those switches show). If their Facebook isn't listed, add it to Accounts Center first. Help: https://www.facebook.com/help/2808345489420767 and, for Reels and Stories, https://help.instagram.com/356902681064399
   - One post at a time instead: before they tap **Share**, tap **Also share on…** and turn on their Page.
5. **Check it worked:** after their next post, open their Facebook Page and look for it there.

### Started on Facebook: make an Instagram account and connect it
1. **Their Facebook Page is set up** (stage 2).
2. **Make their Instagram**, if they don't have one, in the Instagram app: the same name, the same handle if it's free, the same profile photo and their Page's bio (trimmed to Instagram's 150 characters).
3. **Switch it to a professional account:** their profile → the menu (top right) → **Account type and tools** → **Switch to professional account** → a category → **Creator** (right for most digital product sellers). It becomes public. If it offers to connect a Facebook Page, choose theirs, and step 4 is done. Help: https://help.instagram.com/502981923235522
4. **Connect the two**, if step 3 didn't: the same steps as "Started on Instagram", step 3.
5. **Post once, to both:** in **Meta Business Suite** (on a computer, or its app), **Create post**, choose both their Page and their Instagram, then publish. Help: https://www.facebook.com/business/help/266630520743543
   Or post from Instagram with sharing turned on ("Started on Instagram", step 4). For Reels, that's usually the easiest.

### Good to know, both ways
- **Keep Instagram professional.** Switching it back to a personal account disconnects their Page. Help: https://www.facebook.com/business/help/connect-instagram-to-page
- **Their plan still has one home:** every post is made for their main platform, and it simply shows up on the other one too.
- **Comments come in on both,** so check both when they reply (stage 6). Meta Business Suite shows both in one place.

Save it in their notes: **Instagram and Facebook connected:** yes (which way, with the date), not yet, or not using both.

## Then: connect Apify and check the live profile

Once it's live (and Instagram and Facebook are connected, or they chose not yet), it's time for the Apify step (`apify.md`, "Connect it"), if it isn't connected yet. One message: what it does for them, what it costs (free to start), and the 6 steps.
Then run the profile check (`apify.md`, "The profile check") on their live profile, plus one screenshot of the top of it on their phone. A new account with no posts yet gets the short version: the profile parts only.
Fix what it finds, with them, and save it under **Profile checks**. Then stage 2 is done: celebrate, show the roadmap, and hand off to stage 3.
If they'd rather not connect Apify, check the live profile from that one screenshot instead.
