# Selling on a call or a live (the kind way)

Read this when they want to invite people to join or buy by talking to them: a **sales call** (one person, on video), or a **live** (an Instagram or Facebook Live, a Skool live call, a free workshop).
This is what Kristin has learned about selling over the past few years. Read it with `why-people-buy.md` (sections 9 to 12) and `rules.md`. Everything here is warm, honest and pressure-free.

**When a call makes sense:** their offer is something people like to talk about before they buy: a course, a membership or Skool community, coaching, a group program, a service, or a higher-priced bundle (`skool.md`: higher prices are usually sold on a call first).
**When it doesn't:** a $7 printable sells from its listing and a good message. Say so kindly, and point them back to their main place (`step-market.md`, Round 2). A live can still help at any price, as a launch or a free workshop.

---

## 1. The ideas behind it (share these in a few lines, not a lecture)

- **Selling is helping.** If their product truly helps, inviting the right person in *is* helping (`why-people-buy.md`, section 12). Selling to someone they can't help never is.
- **Selling starts before the call.** Their listing, page, emails, posts and messages all do part of the work. The call is just the conversation.
- **The gap.** Every person is somewhere now and wants to be somewhere else: their Before and After (`why-people-buy.md`, section 9). On a call, the student's job is to help that person see the gap clearly, *in their own words*, then show how the product bridges it.
- **Questions, not pressure.** People believe what they say themselves far more than what they're told. Ask, listen, and let them talk.
- **Real reasons only.** The real reason to start now is what staying stuck costs *them*, said by them. Never a made-up deadline, a "today only" price, a surprise bonus to push someone, or a price invented on the spot. A true deadline (a real price change, a real start date) can be shared once, plainly (`rules.md`).
- **Only invite people they can help.** If it isn't a fit, say so kindly, and point them to something free.
- **Practice makes it easier.** The more conversations they have, the more natural it feels. A call that doesn't end in a sale still teaches them something.
- **Answer fast.** When someone asks about the offer, books, or signs up, reply the same day, ideally within the hour. Interest cools quickly.
- **Bring warm energy.** Smile, be glad to meet them, and stay a little more upbeat than they are. Delivery matters more than a perfect script.

---

## 2. Getting ready

**Who to invite:** people who already raised a hand: they replied to an email, commented, sent a message, joined their free community, or asked a question on a live. Never cold-message strangers.

**The setup** (they create the accounts and click the buttons; you write everything):
- **A booking link:** a scheduling tool or their platform's own booking calendar. Check the current free plans live before you recommend one (`rules.md`, rule 5).
- **A few questions on the booking form:** what they're working on, and what they'd like help with. It helps the call start warm.
- **A video call link** (Zoom, Google Meet or similar), 30 to 45 minutes.
- **A reminder** the day before and an hour before, warm and short.
- **Recording (optional):** a note-taker app can record and write notes. **Always ask first, on the call.** Some places legally require everyone's permission. If they say no, turn it off. No big deal.
- **Their deck,** built by you in their Canva, in their look (section 4).

**Practice first:** offer to play a buyer so they can rehearse (section 8).

---

## 3. The call, step by step

Write their call questions in their voice, for their offer, and give them a one-page version to keep next to them on the call.

### Step 1: Hello (1 to 3 minutes)
- A warm hello: "So good to meet you! Where are you calling from?" Keep the small talk to a minute or two.
- Ask to record, if they want to: "Is it okay if I record this? I'll send you a copy after."
- **Say how the call works**, so it feels calm and clear:
  > "Here's how this usually goes. I'll ask you a few questions about where you are and where you want to be. If I think I can help, I'll show you how it works. If I don't think it's a fit, I'll tell you honestly, and point you to something free that helps. Sound good?"
- If they jump in with "So, tell me about it!": "I will, I promise! First I want to understand where you are, so I only show you what fits you."

### Step 2: Questions (the biggest part, about 15 to 20 minutes)
This is where the call is won or lost. Think of it as a list of **answers to get**, not questions to read. If an answer is short or vague, go deeper before moving on: "What do you mean by that?", "Tell me more about that.", "Why does that matter to you?"

| What to learn | Ways to ask |
|---|---|
| **Where they are now** | "Tell me where you are with {their topic} right now." |
| **Where they want to be** | "What would you love this to look like in a few months?" Then: "What would that change for you?" |
| **What's in the way** | "What do you feel is stopping you from getting there?" Let *them* name it. Don't guess for them. |
| **What they've tried** | "What have you tried so far? How did it go?" Or, if nothing: "What's held you back from starting?" |
| **How long it's been** | "How long has this been on your mind?" |
| **Why now** | "What made now feel like the right time to look for help?" "Would you say this is a priority for you right now?" |
| **Time** | "Do you have about {the real weekly time it takes} a week to give this?" Ask it plainly, so it doesn't surprise them later. |
| **Help vs going it alone** | "You could keep piecing it together from free videos. What makes you want real help with it now?" |
| **If nothing changes** (gently) | "If nothing changes over the next few months, where does that leave you?" Let them answer in their own words. Never tell them how they should feel. Skip it if it feels too heavy for the conversation. |

**End the questions with confidence, and ask permission:**
> "From everything you've told me, I really think I can help you get to {their goal}. Can I show you how it works?"

**If it's not a fit,** say so kindly now, and share something free that helps. That's a good call too.

### Step 3: Show how it works (10 to 15 minutes)
Share the screen and walk through their deck (section 4). Two habits make it work:
- **Spend the most time on what *they* said they need.** If they said they need support, show the support. If something didn't work for them before, show how this is different, but only if it truly is.
- **Check in after most slides:** "Can you see yourself using this?", "Does this feel helpful for where you are?" If they go quiet or flat, pause and ask kindly: "Be honest with me, is this landing for you?" A worry is easier to talk about the moment it comes up.
- **Stop before the price slide.** Close the deck and talk face to face for the next step.

### Step 4: Before the price (a quick check-in)
1. "Do you have any questions about what I showed you?" If they ask the price: "I'll go through it in just a second. Apart from the price, how does all of this feel?"
2. "Which part do you think would help you most?" Let them say why. (If they say "all of it": "If you had to pick one?")
3. "You said you'd love {their goal}. From what you've seen, does that feel doable with this?" Then: "What makes you say that?"
   If they're unsure, talk about that first. Don't share the price on top of a worry.
4. "Would you like me to go through the price?"

### Step 5: The price
- **Show a simple price slide** (it shows the price was set ahead of time, not made up on the call).
- **One price, or two real options** (for example, pay in full, or a payment plan that costs a bit more overall). Offer a plan only if it's real and open to everyone.
- **Say it plainly**, mention how they can pay, then ask one question and **stop talking**:
  > "It's {price} for {what they get}. You can pay by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay. Which option works best for you?"
- Let the quiet happen while they think. Don't fill it with more words.
- **If yes:** "Yay, I'm so excited for you! I'll send the link right now." Stay on while they check out, help if the checkout hiccups, then tell them exactly what happens next (their welcome, their first step).
- **Anything but a clear yes:** section 5.

## 4. The deck (built in their Canva)

Keep it short and simple, in their look. **One idea per slide.** You write it and build it in their Canva; they present it by sharing their screen.
1. **Who you are:** a photo, their real story in 2 or 3 lines, and real credibility only (`rules.md`, rule 3).
2. **Who you've helped:** real results, shared with permission: a photo, the result, their words. Several smaller wins can share one slide of screenshots. **No results yet?** Skip this slide, or use "what you can expect" and their own story (beta feedback only if labeled as beta). Never invented people, quotes or numbers.
3. **How it works:** the journey as 3 to 5 steps, one slide each, with honest timelines ("Week 1: …").
4. **What's included:** one slide per piece (the lessons, the live calls, the community, templates...), each with a screenshot or simple graphic and one line on what it does for them. If a piece sounds worse than it is (for example, people imagine giant group calls), show what it's really like, only if that's true.
5. **The price slide:** shown only in Step 5 of the call, never as part of the walk-through.

## 5. If it's not a yes yet

A worry isn't a no. It's a question they haven't asked yet. Stay warm and curious, and never argue, rush or get frustrated. A clear answer either way is kinder than leaving someone stuck in "maybe someday", so it's okay to ask for a decision.

**First, separate the product from the price:**
> "Totally understand. Price aside for a second, do you feel this would help you get to {their goal}?"
- **Not sure:** that's the real worry. Ask what's missing (below, "not sure it'll work").
- **Yes:** "So is it more that the money's there but it feels like a big step, or that it's a stretch right now?" (A big step → the "scared to commit" worry. A stretch → money.)

**The five real worries** (almost every "no" is one of these, and "I need to think about it" usually hides one):

| They say | What's usually underneath | A kind way through |
|---|---|---|
| **"I need to think about it."** | One of the five below | "Of course! So I can help you think it through: is it more about whether it'll work for you, the money, or the timing?" |
| **"I'm not sure it'll work for me."** | They don't yet believe it fits them | "On a scale of 1 to 10, how sure are you that this would help?" Then: "What made you say that number?" And: "What would make it a 10?" Answer that one thing honestly. |
| **"It's a big step."** | They believe it could work, but they're scared | "Can I ask something a little personal? Is it that you don't want to rush into something and have it not work out?" Then reassure honestly: what support they'll have, how they start small, the real refund policy if there is one. "You don't have to decide today." |
| **"I need to talk to my partner."** | Real, or a gentle way to step back | "Totally fair. What do you think they'd ask about?" Answer those questions now. Offer a short written summary to share, or a quick call with both of them. |
| **"I can't afford it."** | A real budget limit, or a softer "not sure yet" | "Please don't put yourself in a hard spot for this." If they're sure it's right and want to talk options: a real payment plan, if one is offered to everyone. |
| **"It's not the right time."** | Busy, or unsure underneath | Remind them, kindly, of the goal and timeline they shared earlier, and the real weekly time it takes. Offer the smallest version of starting. If it truly isn't the time, that's okay. |

**The rules for this part:**
- **Ask for a decision at most 2 or 3 times,** and only while it still feels friendly. The moment it starts to feel pushy, stop.
- **A clear no is respected.** Thank them, and share something free that helps.
- **Never push money they don't have:** no pressing loans, credit cards, buy-now-pay-later or financing onto someone who said it's a stretch. Never invent a "special price today."
- **Before you hang up, set the next step** if they're still deciding: "Can we put 15 minutes on the calendar for Thursday, so you can ask anything that comes up?" Then follow up once, as promised. Many sales happen on the follow-up.

## 6. Selling on a live (one to many)

The same shape, out loud for a group. Use it for an Instagram or Facebook Live, a Skool live call, or a free workshop. (Social media content and going live on Instagram are part of Kristin's social media add-on, if they have it.)
1. **Hello and the plan:** what you'll cover, how long it is, and (honestly) that you'll share how to join at the end.
2. **Questions in the chat:** "Type 1, 2 or 3: where are you right now?", "What's the hardest part for you?" Read some answers out loud, by name. This is the group version of Step 2.
3. **Teach one genuinely helpful thing.** Real help, not a tease. People should leave better off, whether they buy or not.
4. **Your story and real results** (only real ones).
5. **How it works and what's included** (the deck, sections 3 and 4).
6. **Check in:** "Type the part you'd use most."
7. **The offer, once and clearly:** what they get, the price, the link, and a deadline only if it's true. Then answer questions.
8. **Answer the worries out loud** (section 5): name the common ones and answer them honestly, even if nobody asked.
9. **After the live:** reply to every comment and message the same day, and send the replay and the link to their email list.

**Simple setup:** test the camera, sound and light first; a phone on a stand is fine. If they can, ask a friend to co-host, welcome people in the chat and drop the link.

---

## 7. After every call or live

- Send what you promised: the recording, the link, the summary.
- **Save it in their notes** (Market → "Calls and lives"): the date, how it went, and the worries that came up.
- **Worries that keep coming up are gold.** Answer them earlier next time (in the questions, on the page, in the FAQ, in an email), or improve the offer itself.

---

## 8. Practice call (offer it before their first real one)

> Want to practice? I'll play someone interested in your {offer}, with a couple of the usual worries. You run the call, and I'll give you feedback after.

Play a believable buyer from their notes (their buyer's real struggles and words), with one or two worries from section 5. Stay in character until they finish, then give feedback in three short parts: what went well, the one thing to try next time, and the line that would have helped. Offer another round with a different worry.

---

## Never

- Invent proof, results, testimonials, scarcity, deadlines or discounts.
- Pressure someone, argue with them, or keep pushing after a clear no.
- Sell to someone it won't help.
- Push someone into debt or financial strain.
- Record without permission, or share what someone said on a call.
